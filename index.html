What is maintenance readiness?

Maintenance readiness means confirming, before a planned job begins, that three things are true at the same time: the required parts are physically set aside for this job, the assigned technician is free to do it, and production has agreed to hand over the machine.

2 / What we’re buildingKnow what is missing before work begins.

Schdul is maintenance readiness software in development for factory teams. It brings planned maintenance requirements, spare parts status, technician availability and production handover confirmations into one view.

  1. Gather

    Job requirements, parts information and team confirmations for one planned job.

  2. Check

    Find confirmations that are missing, or that something on the floor has since made out of date.

  3. Review

    Show the supervisor what still needs checking. The supervisor decides whether the job goes ahead.

4 / QuestionsAsked plainly.

How is Schdul different from a CMMS or ERP?

A CMMS or ERP stays the system of record for work orders, stock and schedules. Schdul is being built to sit alongside it and check, close to the job, whether those records still hold, and to show who needs to reconfirm what. It does not replace either system.

Can I use Schdul today?

Not yet. The first prototype is in development. Right now we are talking to factory teams about how they check readiness, and those conversations decide what gets built first.

Does Schdul control machines or replace lockout/tagout?

No. Schdul supports readiness checks and supervisor review. It does not control machines, isolate energy or replace your lockout/tagout and permit procedures.

Who decides that a job can start?

Your authorised supervisor. When every check is confirmed, the job is marked ready for supervisor review. It is not approved automatically.

What do you need from me?

One recent planned maintenance job: what was planned, what changed and how your team handled it. No presentation and no confidential records.
